The hard truth

The skills that get you from 110 to 100 aren't the ones that get you from 85 to 79.

Most golfers copy what better players practice. A 100-shooter grinding on wedge distances is like a beginner pianist working on speed. Get the basics that match your level, and the next milestone comes faster.

The math behind each milestone

On a par-72 course, here's what each barrier really takes, hole by hole.

  • Par
  • Bogey
  • Double bogey
  • Triple bogey

Break 120 119

Double bogey on every hole, with eleven triples allowed

Break 100 99

Bogey on every hole, with nine doubles allowed

Break 90 89

Bogey on every hole and one par

Break 80 79

Eleven pars and seven bogeys

Notice that none of these needs a birdie. Each milestone comes from avoiding big numbers, not from making great shots.

Breaking 120

Get the ball in the air and moving forward. Solid, simple contact matters more than distance. A half-swing that goes 100 yards straight beats a full swing that goes 60 yards sideways.

Play the forward tees. Shorter holes mean fewer shots and less frustration while you learn.

Learn to two-putt from close range. Spend practice time on putts inside 15 feet.

Breaking 100

Stop the penalty strokes. Lost balls and water hazards are the biggest score killers at this level. Off the tee, hit the club you trust most, even if it's a hybrid or 5-wood.

Cut your three-putts. Lag putting is the fastest fix. If your first putt from 30 feet finishes within three feet, you'll two-putt almost every time. That's Pillar 3: Putting Adds Up Quickly.

Get out of trouble in one. From the trees, punch back to the fairway. From a bunker, focus only on getting out.

Breaking 90

Take more club into greens. Most golfers at this level come up short on most approaches because they plan for their best swing. That's Pillar 2: Short Is Wrong, and Right Is Long.

Keep chipping simple. Use the least lofted club that will carry the fringe and roll to the hole. Save the 60-degree wedge for when you truly need height. That's Pillar 4: Tipping Your Hat to Chipping.

Turn doubles into bogeys. You don't need more pars. You need fewer doubles. When a hole starts going wrong, play for bogey and move on. That's Pillar 5: Know Thyself.

Breaking 80

Know your wedge distances. Learn how far each wedge carries with a half, three-quarter and full swing. At this level, scoring happens inside 100 yards.

Get up and down more often. Track how often you save par from around the green.

Make the four- to six-footers. These putts decide whether a missed green becomes a par or a bogey.

Aim for the center of greens. Attacking tucked pins brings doubles back into play.

Your four-week plan to break 90

If breaking 90 is your goal for 2027, here's how I'd spend the first month.

  1. Week 1Get your numbersPlay a round and write down putts, penalty strokes, approach shots that finished short, and every double bogey or worse. That's your baseline.
  2. Week 2Club upOn every approach, take one more club than you think and swing smoother. Count how many finish short now.
  3. Week 3Simplify around the greenHit every chip you can with an 8- or 9-iron bump-and-run. Putt from the fringe whenever you can.
  4. Week 4Play for bogeyBefore each tee shot, ask: what's the shot that keeps a double off the card? Play it. Then compare your numbers to week 1.

That's Pillar 6 in action: What Is Your Plan Now? A plan beats a pile of tips every time.

Questions golfers ask

How long does it take to break 90?

It depends on where you start and how often you play, but golfers averaging the low to mid 90s can often get there within a season by fixing short approaches, chipping and blow-up holes.

Do I need lessons to break 90?

Not always. Many golfers get there with a clear plan and better decisions on the course. A coach speeds it up by finding your biggest leak fast.

What if I'm not close to 90 yet?

Start with the milestone in front of you. Breaking 100 first makes breaking 90 a lot easier.

Celebrate the milestone

Breaking a scoring barrier for the first time is one of the best moments in golf. Write the date on the scorecard and keep it. Then pick the next milestone and start again. Make 2027 the year you break 90.
